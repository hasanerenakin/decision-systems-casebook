# Problem Brief

## Case

Internship Placement and Matching System

## Domain

University Career Services and Internship Operations

## Document Status

Approved for Case Development

## Purpose

This document defines the current internship-placement problem, its causes,
institutional effects and target outcomes.

The case focuses on a university career center responsible for connecting
eligible students with approved internship opportunities.

The current process depends heavily on:

- Spreadsheets
- Email messages
- Online forms
- Manually reviewed documents
- Department approval messages
- Employer-provided candidate lists
- Individual staff knowledge

These tools support basic operations but do not provide a unified,
explainable and auditable placement process.

## Background

The university requires or encourages students to complete internships as part
of their academic and professional development.

Internship opportunities are provided by organizations from different
industries, locations and working models.

Each opportunity may define different conditions, including:

- Academic department
- Student year
- Minimum GPA
- Required courses
- Technical skills
- Language proficiency
- Internship dates
- Working model
- Location
- Required documents
- Candidate capacity
- Employer-specific preferences

Students also have different:

- Academic eligibility conditions
- Skills
- Interests
- Career goals
- Location preferences
- Working-model preferences
- Availability periods
- Accessibility requirements
- Financial expectations
- Previous internship experience

The university must consider both sides while also complying with academic
rules, departmental requirements, employer capacity and institutional fairness
principles.

## Current Situation

Internship opportunities are received through multiple channels.

Examples include:

- Employer emails
- Career-center partnerships
- Department contacts
- Online opportunity forms
- Faculty recommendations
- Student-sourced opportunities

Career-center staff manually transfer opportunity information into spreadsheets
or internal lists.

Students usually review opportunities through announcements, email lists or
separate portals.

Applications may then be collected through:

- Email
- Online forms
- Shared spreadsheets
- Employer websites
- Department systems
- Paper documents

Eligibility and compatibility checks are performed manually or through
partially documented practices.

Different staff members may interpret the same requirement differently.

Final placement decisions may depend on:

- Application order
- Staff availability
- Employer feedback
- Academic-department approval
- Informal communication
- Manually calculated comparisons
- Exceptional circumstances

This creates an inconsistent and difficult-to-audit process.

## Main Problem Statement

The university does not have a unified system for evaluating internship
eligibility, comparing student and employer requirements, managing opportunity
capacity and producing explainable placement recommendations.

As a result, placement decisions require significant manual effort and may be
inconsistent, delayed or difficult to explain.

## Observed Problems

### 1. Fragmented Student Information

Student information may be distributed across:

- Student information systems
- CV documents
- Application forms
- Department records
- Email messages
- Career-center spreadsheets

This makes it difficult to determine whether a student profile is complete and
current.

### 2. Inconsistent Academic Eligibility Checks

Academic eligibility may depend on:

- Department
- Academic year
- Completed credits
- Required courses
- GPA
- Previous internship status
- Department approval
- Internship period

These conditions are not always checked through a standard process.

A student may therefore:

- Apply before becoming eligible
- Be rejected after a long review period
- Receive conflicting information from different units
- Submit an application without required academic approval

### 3. Unstructured Employer Requirements

Employers may describe candidate requirements using unstructured text.

Examples include:

- Strong communication skills
- Good knowledge of office software
- Interest in data analysis
- Preferably third-year students
- Advanced English
- Available for on-site work

These requirements may not be clearly classified as:

- Mandatory
- Preferred
- Optional

This makes consistent candidate evaluation difficult.

### 4. Manual Candidate Comparison

Career-center staff may compare students and opportunities manually.

This requires reviewing:

- Student profiles
- CVs
- Academic information
- Skills
- Employer requirements
- Preferences
- Availability
- Application status

As application volume increases, manual comparison becomes slower and more
error-prone.

### 5. Limited Consideration of Student Preferences

A student may be academically eligible for an opportunity but may not prefer:

- The location
- The industry
- The working model
- The internship period
- The role type
- The compensation conditions
- The company size

When preferences are not considered consistently, students may reject offers
or complete placements with low satisfaction.

### 6. Employer Capacity Problems

Each employer or opportunity may accept a limited number of students.

Capacity may become inaccurate when:

- Multiple staff members update separate files
- A student declines an offer
- An employer reduces available positions
- An offer remains unanswered
- A placement is cancelled
- A student accepts another opportunity

This may result in:

- Duplicate offers
- Over-allocation
- Unused capacity
- Delayed reassignment
- Incorrect opportunity availability

### 7. Unequal Opportunity Distribution

Some students may receive multiple recommendations or offers while others
receive none.

Possible reasons include:

- Stronger or more complete profiles
- Earlier application timing
- Popular departments
- Employer preference patterns
- Staff familiarity with certain students
- Incomplete student information
- Limited opportunity availability
- Repeated recommendation of the same candidates

The university currently has limited visibility into whether opportunities are
distributed consistently.

### 8. Unexplained Placement Decisions

Students and departments may not understand why:

- A student was considered ineligible
- Another candidate was ranked higher
- A recommendation was rejected
- A manual override was applied
- An employer did not receive a student profile
- A placement was assigned to another student

Without structured decision reasons, the process may appear arbitrary.

### 9. Delayed Decisions

Placement may be delayed by:

- Missing documents
- Late department approval
- Employer response time
- Manual profile comparison
- Incomplete capacity information
- Repeated email communication
- Unclear responsibility
- Conflicting internship dates

Delays may cause students to miss academic deadlines or lose opportunities.

### 10. Duplicate and Conflicting Placements

A student may:

- Apply to several opportunities
- Receive multiple offers
- Accept one offer without updating another application
- Be proposed to multiple employers
- Be placed by both a department and the career center

Without a unified placement status, conflicting commitments may occur.

### 11. Limited Monitoring of Unplaced Students

Career-center staff may focus on active applications without having a clear
view of students who:

- Have no eligible opportunity
- Have received no recommendation
- Have repeated rejections
- Have incomplete profiles
- Have restrictive preferences
- Are approaching an academic deadline
- Require additional support

These students may remain unnoticed until the placement period is nearly over.

### 12. Weak Historical Learning

Previous placement data may not be used systematically to understand:

- Which employers regularly accept students
- Which skills are frequently requested
- Which departments face capacity shortages
- Which students are more likely to decline offers
- Which placements result in successful completion
- Which recommendation patterns are ineffective
- Which employers repeatedly cancel opportunities

This limits continuous improvement.

## Root Causes

The observed problems are connected to several underlying causes.

### Process Causes

- No standardized end-to-end placement workflow
- Different departmental practices
- Unclear decision ownership
- Informal exception handling
- Manual status updates
- Separate approval processes

### Data Causes

- Fragmented student records
- Missing or outdated profile information
- Unstructured employer requirements
- Inconsistent skill terminology
- Duplicate applications
- Inaccurate capacity values
- Limited historical outcome data

### Technology Causes

- Reliance on spreadsheets and email
- No unified application status
- No standard eligibility engine
- No explainable matching mechanism
- Limited integration between university systems
- No centralized audit history

### Governance Causes

- Unclear override rules
- Inconsistent eligibility interpretation
- Limited fairness monitoring
- Insufficient access controls
- No standard decision-reason categories
- Weak responsibility separation

### Operational Causes

- High application volume
- Limited career-center staff
- Employer response delays
- Short placement periods
- Changing employer capacity
- Student preference changes
- Academic-calendar constraints

## Problem Boundaries

The system is intended to support internship placement and matching.

It is not intended to replace:

- Academic-department authority
- Employer hiring decisions
- Student consent
- Legal review
- Accessibility assessment
- Human judgment in exceptional cases

The system may recommend and organize decisions, but final placement requires
authorized human review and agreement between relevant parties.

## Target State

The university should have a unified process in which:

1. Student profiles use standardized information.
2. Academic eligibility is evaluated consistently.
3. Employer requirements are classified clearly.
4. Mandatory requirements are separated from preferences.
5. Student preferences are considered during matching.
6. Opportunity capacity is updated centrally.
7. Ineligible combinations are excluded before ranking.
8. Eligible combinations receive explainable compatibility results.
9. Career-center staff review placement recommendations.
10. Overrides require documented reasons.
11. Students and employers receive clear status information.
12. Unplaced students are identified early.
13. Placement outcomes are monitored.
14. Historical results support future planning.

## Target Outcomes

### Outcome 1: Reduced Manual Review

Career-center staff should spend less time performing repetitive eligibility
and candidate-comparison tasks.

### Outcome 2: Improved Eligibility Accuracy

Invalid student-opportunity combinations should be identified before they
reach the final placement stage.

### Outcome 3: Better Matching Quality

Recommendations should consider both employer requirements and student
preferences.

### Outcome 4: Improved Capacity Control

Opportunity capacity should reflect:

- Available positions
- Pending offers
- Accepted offers
- Declined offers
- Confirmed placements
- Cancelled placements

### Outcome 5: Earlier Support for Unplaced Students

The system should identify students who are at risk of remaining unplaced
before critical deadlines.

### Outcome 6: Explainable Decisions

Each recommendation, exclusion, approval, rejection and override should include
a documented reason.

### Outcome 7: Improved Auditability

Important decisions and changes should preserve:

- User or system identity
- Timestamp
- Previous status
- New status
- Decision reason
- Related student
- Related opportunity

### Outcome 8: Measurable Fairness

The university should be able to compare placement and recommendation outcomes
across relevant groups and investigate significant differences.

Fairness indicators should support human review and should not be treated as
automatic proof of discrimination.

## Preliminary Success Criteria

The future system should support the following measurable objectives.

| Success Area | Preliminary Target |
|---|---|
| Academic eligibility validation | 100% of submitted applications evaluated |
| Mandatory requirement validation | 100% before recommendation |
| Placement decision traceability | 100% of final decisions documented |
| Manual override documentation | 100% with a reason |
| Duplicate confirmed placements | 0 active conflicts |
| Opportunity capacity visibility | Updated after every offer decision |
| Unplaced-student identification | Before the defined intervention deadline |
| Recommendation explanation | Available for every generated recommendation |
| Personal-data access | Restricted by authorized role |
| Historical placement outcomes | Retained for approved reporting periods |

These are preliminary targets and may be revised after detailed requirements,
risk analysis and stakeholder validation.

## Constraints

### Academic Constraints

- Departments may have different eligibility rules.
- Internship periods may depend on the academic calendar.
- Exceptional academic approval may be required.
- Mandatory and voluntary internships may use different processes.

### Employer Constraints

- Employers may change requirements.
- Employers may reduce or cancel capacity.
- Some employers may use their own application systems.
- Employer decisions may not include detailed reasons.

### Student Constraints

- Students may update preferences.
- Students may decline offers.
- Students may provide incomplete profiles.
- Availability may change during the placement period.

### Technical Constraints

- University systems may not provide real-time integrations.
- Some data may require manual validation.
- Skill terminology may be inconsistent.
- Historical data may be incomplete.

### Governance Constraints

- Personal data must be protected.
- Automated placement decisions are not permitted in the initial design.
- Overrides require authorization and traceability.
- Sensitive student circumstances should not be exposed unnecessarily.

## Assumptions

The initial case assumes:

- Students have authenticated university accounts.
- Academic program data is available.
- Employers and opportunities are reviewed before activation.
- Each opportunity has a defined capacity.
- Students provide consent for relevant profile use.
- Career-center staff have authority to confirm placement workflow status.
- Academic departments retain authority over academic exceptions.
- Employers retain authority over candidate acceptance.
- Final placement requires student acceptance.
- Recommendation scores are advisory rather than binding.

## Problem Statement Summary

The current internship-placement process is fragmented, manual and difficult
to audit.

Student eligibility, employer requirements, preferences, opportunity capacity
and placement decisions are managed across multiple tools and organizational
units.

The proposed system should create a unified and explainable placement process
that:

- Validates eligibility consistently
- Structures employer requirements
- Considers student preferences
- Tracks capacity accurately
- Generates transparent recommendations
- Preserves authorized human review
- Identifies unplaced students early
- Records decision history
- Measures placement outcomes

The next document will identify the stakeholders, their responsibilities,
information needs, conflicts and influence on the system.
