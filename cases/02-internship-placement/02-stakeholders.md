# Stakeholder Analysis

## Case

Internship Placement and Matching System

## Domain

University Career Services and Internship Operations

## Document Purpose

This document identifies the stakeholders involved in the internship
placement process and analyzes their:

- Objectives
- Responsibilities
- Information needs
- Decision authority
- System interactions
- Concerns
- Potential conflicts
- Influence on project outcomes

The placement process involves multiple parties whose goals are related but
not always identical.

A successful system must balance:

- Student preferences
- Academic requirements
- Employer expectations
- Career-center workload
- Opportunity capacity
- Institutional fairness
- Privacy
- Explainability
- Human decision authority

## Stakeholder Groups

The main stakeholder groups are:

### Primary Stakeholders

- Students
- Employers
- Career-center staff
- Academic departments
- Internship coordinators

### Decision and Governance Stakeholders

- Career-center management
- Faculty or department internship committees
- University administration
- Legal and privacy teams
- Information security team
- Internal control or audit units

### Supporting Stakeholders

- University IT team
- Student information system administrators
- Document-management administrators
- Notification-service administrators
- Accessibility and student-support services
- Alumni and employer-relations teams

# Detailed Stakeholder Analysis

## 1. Students

### Role

Students create profiles, review internship opportunities, submit preferences,
apply for available positions, respond to placement offers and report
internship outcomes.

### Main Objectives

Students want to:

- Find internships related to their academic program
- Gain relevant professional experience
- Be considered for preferred opportunities
- Understand eligibility and rejection reasons
- Avoid missing deadlines
- Protect their personal information
- Receive equal and transparent treatment
- Complete mandatory internship requirements
- Track application and placement status
- Communicate changing preferences or availability

### Responsibilities

Students are responsible for:

- Providing accurate profile information
- Maintaining current contact details
- Declaring relevant skills honestly
- Submitting required documents
- Selecting opportunity preferences
- Reporting availability constraints
- Responding to offers before expiration
- Informing the university about external placements
- Completing required evaluations
- Reporting placement cancellation or withdrawal

### Information Needs

Students need access to:

- Opportunity descriptions
- Mandatory and preferred requirements
- Eligibility results
- Missing document warnings
- Application deadlines
- Application status
- Recommendation explanations
- Offer conditions
- Acceptance deadlines
- Placement confirmation
- Internship-completion requirements
- Contact and escalation channels

### Main Concerns

- Being rejected without explanation
- Receiving irrelevant recommendations
- Missing an opportunity because of outdated information
- Personal data being shared unnecessarily
- Employer requirements being applied inconsistently
- Manual overrides favoring other students
- Restrictive preferences reducing available opportunities
- Technical problems affecting deadlines
- Being matched to an inaccessible location or working model
- Accepting an offer that conflicts with academic requirements

### Decision Authority

Students have authority to:

- Submit or withdraw applications
- Rank preferences
- Accept or decline offers
- Update permitted profile fields
- Withdraw consent where applicable
- Report incorrect information
- Request review of an eligibility or placement decision

Students do not have authority to:

- Change academic eligibility rules
- Modify employer requirements
- Change opportunity capacity
- Approve their own exception
- Confirm a placement without institutional and employer approval

### Success Indicators

Student success may be reflected through:

- Placement rate
- First-preference placement rate
- Offer acceptance rate
- Placement satisfaction
- Successful internship completion
- Reduced decision-response time
- Reduced number of unresolved eligibility disputes

---

## 2. Employer Representatives

### Role

Employer representatives register organizations, publish internship
opportunities, define candidate requirements, review candidates, communicate
decisions and confirm accepted placements.

### Main Objectives

Employers want to:

- Receive suitable and eligible candidates
- Reduce irrelevant applications
- Fill internship capacity efficiently
- Communicate requirements clearly
- Review candidates within a manageable process
- Maintain a positive relationship with the university
- Track offer and placement status
- Avoid repeated candidate submissions
- Receive timely responses from students

### Responsibilities

Employers are responsible for:

- Providing accurate organization information
- Defining internship responsibilities
- Classifying mandatory and preferred requirements
- Declaring opportunity capacity
- Maintaining current internship dates
- Identifying working model and location
- Reviewing candidates within agreed periods
- Recording acceptance or rejection outcomes
- Reporting changes or cancellations
- Providing completion feedback where required

### Information Needs

Employers need access to:

- Approved opportunity records
- Eligible candidate profiles
- Relevant student skills
- Academic program information
- Student availability
- Application status
- Offer-response status
- Remaining opportunity capacity
- University contact information
- Placement-confirmation information

Employer access should be limited to information necessary for candidate
evaluation and placement administration.

### Main Concerns

- Receiving ineligible candidates
- Incomplete or inaccurate student profiles
- Delayed candidate lists
- Students declining after long review periods
- Opportunity capacity being exceeded
- Sensitive student information being shared unnecessarily
- University staff changing requirements without approval
- Duplicate submissions of the same student
- Difficulty understanding university internship rules
- Lack of response after an employer decision

### Decision Authority

Employers may:

- Define opportunity requirements
- Review eligible candidates
- Accept or reject candidates
- Update opportunity capacity before controlled deadlines
- Withdraw an opportunity with a documented reason
- Confirm placement participation

Employers may not:

- Modify university academic eligibility
- Access unrelated student records
- view protected student information without authorization
- confirm an institutional placement without required university approval
- silently change accepted conditions after placement confirmation

### Success Indicators

Employer success may be reflected through:

- Opportunity fill rate
- Candidate acceptance rate
- Average candidate-review time
- Employer satisfaction
- Repeat employer participation
- Internship completion rate
- Reduced number of ineligible applications

## 3. Career-Center Specialists

### Role

Career-center specialists coordinate the operational placement process.

They validate information, review recommendations, resolve exceptions,
communicate with stakeholders and maintain placement records.

### Main Objectives

Career-center specialists want to:

- Reduce repetitive manual work
- View complete student and opportunity information
- Identify missing or conflicting records quickly
- Review explainable matching results
- Track opportunity capacity accurately
- Identify students requiring intervention
- Manage deadlines and pending actions
- Communicate decisions consistently
- Preserve decision history

### Responsibilities

Career-center specialists are responsible for:

- Reviewing employer registrations
- Validating internship opportunities
- Monitoring student-profile completeness
- Reviewing matching recommendations
- Recording approval or rejection decisions
- Coordinating with academic departments
- Managing offer and placement status
- Escalating fairness or privacy concerns
- Supporting students who remain unplaced
- Recording manual overrides
- Maintaining communication history where required

### Information Needs

Career-center specialists need access to:

- Student profile status
- Academic eligibility result
- Opportunity details
- Mandatory and preferred requirements
- Compatibility indicators
- Student preferences
- Employer capacity
- Application history
- Placement-offer status
- Decision explanations
- Missing-document warnings
- Unplaced-student alerts
- Audit history

### Main Concerns

- Excessive recommendation volume
- Incomplete student profiles
- Unstructured employer requirements
- Multiple systems showing different statuses
- Manual overrides without clear policy
- Pressure to make rapid decisions
- Capacity becoming outdated
- Difficulty explaining results to students
- Important deadlines being missed
- Sensitive data being visible to unauthorized users

### Decision Authority

Depending on university policy, career-center specialists may:

- Approve standard opportunities
- Review recommendation evidence
- Create placement offers
- Reject incomplete applications
- Place recommendations on hold
- Request additional documents
- Resolve standard operational conflicts
- Escalate exceptional cases

They may require higher authorization to:

- Approve academic exceptions
- Override mandatory requirements
- Change matching policies
- access sensitive student circumstances
- approve high-impact or fairness-sensitive decisions

### Success Indicators

- Reduced average review time
- Lower manual correction rate
- Higher data completeness
- Fewer unresolved applications
- Earlier detection of unplaced students
- Complete decision-reason documentation
- Accurate opportunity-capacity records

### Responsibilities

Academic advisors are responsible for:

- Reviewing academic eligibility
- Confirming course or credit requirements
- Evaluating exceptional cases
- Providing academic approval or rejection

### Information Needs

Academic advisors need access to:

- Academic program
- Current year
- GPA where applicable
- Completed and missing courses
- Internship history
- Opportunity responsibilities
- Internship dates
- Employer and role information
- Academic eligibility result
- Exception-request evidence

### Main Concerns

- Students being placed in academically irrelevant roles

### Decision Authority

Academic advisors may:

- Confirm standard eligibility

- Change employer capacity
- approve placement without student consent

- modify institution-wide policies independently

# Stakeholder Conflicts

## Conflict 1: Student Preference vs Employer Requirements

A student may strongly prefer an opportunity but fail to meet a mandatory
employer requirement.

### Resolution Principle

Mandatory conditions must be evaluated before preference ranking.

The system should explain the failed condition rather than present the
opportunity as a low-scoring match.

---

## Conflict 2: Student Choice vs University Eligibility

A student may obtain an external internship that does not meet academic
requirements.

### Resolution Principle

The system should distinguish:

- Employer acceptance
- University placement approval
- Academic internship-credit approval

One status must not automatically imply the others.

---

## Conflict 3: Employer Selectivity vs Student Access

Employers may prefer a narrow candidate profile, which could reduce opportunity
access for many students.

### Resolution Principle

The system should preserve legitimate mandatory requirements while allowing the
university to monitor:

- Rejection patterns
- Candidate concentration
- Department access
- Opportunity availability

Employer requirements should not be silently changed by the university.

---

## Conflict 4: Placement Rate vs Placement Quality

The university may want a high placement rate, while students and departments
may prioritize relevance and quality.

### Resolution Principle

Performance should not be measured only through placement rate.

The KPI framework should also include:

- Preference alignment
- Employer acceptance
- Completion
- Student satisfaction
- Academic relevance
- Placement sustainability

---

## Conflict 5: Automation Efficiency vs Human Judgment

Career-center staff may want faster automated matching, while academic,
fairness and accessibility concerns require contextual review.

### Resolution Principle

The system should automate:

- Validation
- Exclusion-rule checks
- Compatibility indicators
- Capacity tracking
- Alerts

Final placement should remain human reviewed.

---

## Conflict 6: Transparency vs Privacy

Students need understandable decisions, but explanations must not expose:

- Other candidates' information
- Confidential employer evaluations
- Sensitive personal records
- Internal security rules

### Resolution Principle

The system should explain decisions using the student's own eligibility,
requirements and status without revealing unrelated personal or confidential
information.

---

## Conflict 7: Department Autonomy vs Institutional Consistency

Departments may have different internship policies.

Excessive variation may make the process confusing and difficult to operate.

### Resolution Principle

The system should support:

- Shared institutional rules
- Clearly versioned department-specific rules
- Documented exceptions
- Identified policy owners
- Effective dates

---

## Conflict 8: Employer Capacity vs Multiple Student Offers

Employers may want to review several candidates for a limited number of
positions.

Students may also receive multiple offers.

### Resolution Principle

The system should distinguish between:

- Opportunity capacity
- Candidate-review capacity
- Pending offers
- Reserved positions
- Accepted offers
- Confirmed placements

Capacity should not be reduced permanently before the relevant reservation or
acceptance stage.

# Escalation Model

## Operational Escalation

Used for:

- Missing documents
- Pending employer responses
- Expiring offers
- Capacity conflicts
- Duplicate applications
- Unconfirmed placements

Primary escalation owner:

- Career-center specialist
- Career-center manager when overdue

## Academic Escalation

Used for:

- Eligibility disputes
- Course exceptions
- Internship relevance concerns
- Academic deadline risks
- Conflicting department rules

Primary escalation owner:

- Department internship coordinator
- Authorized academic committee when required

## Privacy or Security Escalation

Used for:

- Unauthorized access
- Personal-data exposure
- Suspicious exports
- Manipulated decisions
- Compromised accounts
- Missing audit records

Primary escalation owner:

- Privacy team
- Information security team
- System owner

## Fairness Escalation

Used for:

- Significant placement-rate differences
- Repeated concentration of recommendations
- Unexplained override patterns
- Groups receiving no recommendations
- Employer requirements producing unexpected exclusion patterns

Primary escalation owner:

- Career-center governance
- Relevant academic and institutional review roles

Fairness indicators should trigger investigation rather than automatic
conclusions.

# Stakeholder Success Criteria

The stakeholder design will be considered successful when:

- Students can understand their application and eligibility status.
- Employers receive candidates who satisfy mandatory requirements.
- Career-center staff can review recommendations with supporting evidence.
- Academic departments retain authority over academic eligibility.
- Opportunity capacity remains accurate throughout the offer lifecycle.
- Manual overrides require authorization and explanation.
- Sensitive information is restricted by purpose and role.
- Unplaced students can be identified before critical deadlines.
- Administrators receive reliable aggregated reports.
- Audit teams can reconstruct important decisions.
- IT teams can manage integrations and rule versions consistently.
- Accessibility-related cases can receive authorized human review.

## Stakeholder Analysis Summary

The internship-placement process involves stakeholders with different goals
and decision rights.

Students prioritize suitable and fair opportunities.

Employers prioritize qualified and available candidates.

Academic departments prioritize academic validity.

Career-center teams prioritize operational effectiveness, placement outcomes
and process consistency.

Privacy, security and audit teams prioritize responsible and traceable system
operation.

The proposed system must therefore avoid treating placement as a simple
automated ranking problem.

It must support:

- Eligibility validation
- Structured requirements
- Preference-aware recommendations
- Capacity management
- Human decision-making
- Department authority
- Student consent
- Privacy protection
- Explainability
- Auditability
- Escalation and appeal processes

The next document will translate these stakeholder needs into functional,
non-functional, data, security and governance requirements.
